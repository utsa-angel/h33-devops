Willow

Willow (Salix) is a genus of deciduous trees and shrubs in the family Salicaceae, native to temperate and cold regions of the Northern Hemisphere. Recent classifications recognize around 350 to 400 species, which are notorious for cross-hybridizing, traditionally divided into several subgenera including Salix and Vetrix.

The Salix subgenus includes the true willows, often characterized by long, narrow leaves and tree-like habits, found heavily in riparian zones. The Vetrix subgenus, or sallows, are typically more shrub-like with broader leaves and distinctively silky catkins that appear before early spring growth. The weeping willow (Salix babylonica) is the most commonly cultivated ornamental species.
